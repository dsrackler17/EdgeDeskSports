Methodology and limitations

    What each number means

    • Right winner: the side EdgeDesk gave over a 50% chance won. Games at exactly 50% and ties are not counted.
    • Brier score: the average squared gap between the win probability and what happened (1 or 0). Lower is better; always saying 50% scores 0.25.
    • Log loss: the same idea, punishing a confident miss harder. Lower is better; always saying 50% scores 0.693.
    • Margin and total error: how far the final margin (home minus away) and the final total landed from EdgeDesk’s projection, in points (goals for the NHL). MAE is the average distance; bias is the average signed miss.
    • Sample states: under 50 graded games is too early to read; 50 to 199 is an early signal; 200 to 499 is developing; 500 or more is a meaningful sample. Nothing here is a guarantee of what comes next.

    Limitations

    • Small samples swing. A week, or a sport early in its season, says little on its own.
    • Final scores are noisy: a late score or overtime moves a margin without saying much about the forecast.
    • The ledger captures EdgeDesk’s published forecasts hourly. A number revised in the last hour before a start may not be the one graded; the graded one is always a genuine pregame forecast.
    • Market lines are named-book lines captured by EdgeDesk; they are not every book, and they are not closing lines unless labelled so.
    • MLB, college baseball and UFC have no published per-game forecast, so nothing is graded for them. Retired sports are not shown.

    The ledger, the grades and the summary are public files: summary.json. Every ledger row is hash-chained to the one before it, so a changed row breaks the chain. See also the public record of EdgeDesk’s flagged edges and recommendations, and how EdgeDesk builds a number.
