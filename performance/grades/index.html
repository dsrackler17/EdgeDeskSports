EdgeDesk Grade Performance

EdgeDesk gives every game it researches an A–F Research Grade: how complete, current, cross-checked and reliable the pregame research is. This page checks how the forecasts behind each grade held up once the games were played. A grade is not the chance a side wins and not a betting recommendation.

    How research grades are made, and how they are checked

    What a grade measures

    A Research Grade rates the quality and reliability of EdgeDesk’s pregame research on a game. It is a deterministic score from 0 to 100, built from five parts. The weights are a provisional starting configuration, not a validated finding:

    • Model reliability and calibration (30). The model’s own live record of pregame forecasts graded before this one (Brier skill and calibration), and its walk-forward validation state (retrospective, labelled so). Fewer than 50 graded games: unvalidated, excluded and the grade is capped at C; 50–199: counted, capped at B.
    • Data completeness (25). The model outputs a sport should publish, how fresh the build is against its own limit, the engine’s own input-quality measure (for college football, its 0–100 reliability score) and the authority of the data sources.
    • Model–market comparison (20). A named-book line captured before kickoff, its age, and how far EdgeDesk’s spread, total and win probability sit from it. A large unexplained gap lowers the grade: with a liquid market, it is more often a data problem than an opportunity. No line captured: the part is excluded and the grade is capped at B.
    • Matchup clarity (15). Whether the key players (quarterbacks, goalies, stars) are confirmed, how far one absence would move the number, whether the factors behind the number are itemised, and whether the start time is confirmed. Unknown key-player availability caps the grade at C.
    • Uncertainty (10). How much the model’s variants disagree or how stable the number is under perturbation, its calibration band, and whether ratings are early-season.

    Letters: A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F below 60, on the score rounded to the nearest whole number. Not graded when a prerequisite fails: no model win probability, a number produced after kickoff, an expired build, an invalidated projection (such as a starting-quarterback conflict), a model that reads market prices, a research state of “data insufficient”, or too little data.

    Missing data

    • Nothing missing earns credit. A part with no data scores zero and is named in the Grade Breakdown with the reason.
    • Only a question that does not arise for the sport leaves the calculation (the NHL publishes no margin, so its puck line is not compared with one).
    • The two kinds of evidence the method treats separately, an unvalidated live track record and a missing sportsbook line, are excluded and capped rather than credited. The cap is the price of the missing evidence, and no weight can lift it.

    How results are tracked

      What each number means

      • Brier score: the average squared gap between EdgeDesk’s win probability and what happened (1 or 0). Lower is better; always saying 50% scores 0.250.
      • Calibration: whether games EdgeDesk called 70% were won about 70% of the time, bucket by bucket, each with a Wilson 95% interval.
      • MAE and RMSE: the average miss of the projected margin (home minus away) and total, in points (goals for the NHL). RMSE weighs big misses more.
      • Model agreement and data completeness: the average of those two parts of the grade, 0–100, for the games in the row.
      • Against the spread and over/under: the side EdgeDesk’s number sat on against the named-book line captured with the grade before kickoff, and how it landed. A research comparison, not a betting record, and never chosen from the final.
      • Closing-line value: only against a close from the same source as the line the grade saw; otherwise it is not shown.
      • Intervals and evidence: a difference between two grades is called evidence only when its 95% interval excludes zero, with at least 50 settled games on each side. Until then this page says there is no evidence yet.

      Limitations

      • The grade ledger began recently. Until enough games are settled, this page is honestly in its insufficient-data state, and no grade is claimed to predict anything.
      • The weights and reference points are provisional. A change to them is a new grade version: earlier grades keep their own version and are never re-scored.
      • Research quality is not betting profitability. An A-grade game can be a poor bet and an F-grade game can win; the grade says how much the research can carry.
      • Final scores are noisy. One game, a week or a sport early in its season says little on its own.
      • Grades cover the sports EdgeDesk publishes a per-game forecast for: NFL, college football, NBA, NHL, WNBA and college basketball. MLB, college baseball and UFC have none, so they are not graded.
      • Completed games from before the grade ledger began have no pregame grade and are never given one afterwards. A retrospective analysis, if one is ever published, will be labelled as such and kept apart.

      The data are public files: summary.json and the game files it lists; every grade row in the ledger is hash-chained to the one before it. See also Model Performance, the full forecast record, and how EdgeDesk builds a number.
