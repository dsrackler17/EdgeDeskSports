## What the line archive changed

The v1 football engine in `football/` states that no public historical CFB
betting-line archive exists, and therefore makes no market claim for CFB at
all. That statement is now falsified: `sportsdataverse/cfbfastR-data` carries
`betting/csv/cfb_line_odds.csv.gz` — 1.18M rows, 2006-2025, spread, total and
moneyline, **opening and closing**, across multiple books including Pinnacle.
Coverage of completed FBS-vs-FBS games is 94-100% from 2012 on.

So this model can be, and is, held to the market. The result above is what
being held to it looks like.

## What was offered to the model and REJECTED by it

These are the received wisdoms this data does not support. Each was fitted
honestly and came back at or near zero:

- **Per-venue home-field advantage.** The obvious estimator — each stadium's
  own shrunk mean residual — correlates **0.771** with the home team's own rating.
  Because a team plays every home game at one stadium, whatever the ratings have
  not yet absorbed about that team lands on its venue: the "top venues" list comes
  out reading like a top-25 poll. On held-out seasons it moved MAE from 13.2891 to 13.271 —
  0.018 of a point, bought by absorbing team quality into stadium names. The table
  does not ship; the single league constant does.
- **Player development (Section VII).** A program's efficiency above what its
  experience, continuity and returning production predict does **not repeat**:
  year-over-year correlation of the development delta is **0.0445** over 690 program
  pairs. "This staff develops players" is not visible in this data, so no
  per-program development value ships. (Recruiting pedigree is absent from the
  baseline, so this measures development on top of continuity, not on top of talent.)
- **Travel.** r2 = 0.001976 on 6419 road games (mean trip 512.3 miles). Distance, time
  zones and altitude barely move the mean once team strength is accounted for.
- **Rivalry volatility.** 467 detected pairs; only **42%** of them are actually
  more volatile than a typical game. Rivalry games are not reliably wilder.
- **Roster turnover, youth, rivalry, travel, scoring environment and mismatch as
  volatility drivers.** All seven were offered to a maximum-likelihood sigma fit;
  it kept exactly one: early_season. Everything else was driven to zero.

The surviving volatility model is **directionally correct but weak** out of sample: rank correlation between
predicted sigma and realised absolute error is 0.0442 over 3111 games. The engine still
publishes a volatility index, and it is labelled a research signal rather than a
validated one.

Conditioned on the market number — which the line archive makes possible —
**P(margin = 3 | spread = 3) = 6.66%** in the Power 4, against the **8.8%** this
repo ships for the NFL. The three is real in college football, but it is worth
appreciably less, and a model that reuses NFL key-number mass mis-prices every
field-goal-sized spread.

There are also **no ties**: zero of 17,472 FBS-vs-FBS games 2001-2025 finished level.
The shipped conditional tables assign exactly zero mass to a 0-point margin, so a
pick'em never gets a fabricated push.
