▪ Uncertainty, on the page

The model is allowed to say “I don’t know.”

Most betting products are built to always produce an answer. EdgeDesk is built to tell you when its answer is not trustworthy yet — on the game, in the place you would otherwise have trusted it.

Unknown quarterback

No public college feed publishes a starter EdgeDesk trusts. When it does not know, it says so and carries the last known value — never a guess. Unknown is not healthy.

Missing data

A column that fails its coverage gate is declared missing league-wide rather than scored as if the events did not happen. Missing is not zero.

Unvalidated layers

Player quality and scheme have not cleared walk-forward validation, so they move no projected line anywhere in the product. They are labelled research, and they stay there.

Stale market

When the only price on file is an old capture, nothing is compared until a fresh quote lands. A gap against a price that no longer exists is not a gap.

A very large gap

Past the guard bound, EdgeDesk reads its own number as unsafe rather than as an opportunity. Missing information is the likelier explanation until the gap is explained.

Thin data

A projection the model has little confidence in is marked thin and read as provisional — not as agreement with the market, and not as disagreement either.

▪ Three separate checks

The price. The model. The books.
Never blended.

Each one answers a different question, from a different source. Each one is graded on its own.

The price

Is this sportsbook charging less than everyone else?

EdgeDesk takes the house margin out of every sportsbook’s number, anchors to the book whose prices move fastest, and compares. Then it grades itself on where the price finished before kickoff.

The model

Does EdgeDesk’s own model see the game differently?

A separate football engine that projects the game from scratch. Treated as experimental until its published record earns it more than that.

The books

Do the sportsbooks disagree with each other?

How far apart the books are on the same game. That is a measure of how settled the number is — not a prediction of anything.

These three are kept apart on purpose. EdgeDesk never combines them into a single score, and never lets one borrow confidence from another, because agreement manufactured from unrelated systems is not evidence.

Division of labor

Use whatever AI you already use.

The only scoreboard that isn't luck

Winning tonight doesn't prove your process works. Did you get the better price?

The idea in one line: just before a game starts, every sportsbook has settled on its final number. If the price you took was better than that final number, you bought well — whatever happened in the game.

That final number is the market's most accurate price, and beating it is the only signal that exists before results do. Bettors call it closing line value. If you consistently bought a better number than the market settled on, your process is finding something. If you didn't, a winning night was luck wearing a disguise.

▪ Research workflow

Six steps. Every night.

  1. 1Scan

    Find an unusual price or model disagreement.

  2. 2Verify

    Check the benchmark sportsbook, how many books are quoting it, how the price has moved, and whether the data holds up.

  3. 3Research

    Study the matchup, roster, stats, model context and news.

  4. 4Decide

    PASS, WATCH, or research further.

  5. 5Track

    Freeze the price in the Ledger.

  6. 6Grade

    Compare the entry against the closing market.
