Wind hits pass-heavy offenses harder, and totals lag behind

In 5,203 outdoor NFL games, every 10 mph of wind costs passing efficiency, costs pass-heavy offenses more, and lowers scoring by more than the total line moves.

Candidate signal, unvalidatedNFL · 1999–2025 · outdoor gamesUpdated 2026-10-17
Research questionDoes a 20 mph wind hurt a pass-heavy offense more than a run-heavy one, and does the betting market already account for it?

Findings

  • Each 10 mph of wind lowers EPA per dropback by 0.039 for a team with a league-average passing identity (95% CI 0.029 to 0.048).
  • The penalty grows with pass-heaviness: a further 0.027 EPA per dropback per 10 mph for each 10 points of neutral-situation pass rate above league (p = 0.007). Pass-heavy teams score 1.9 fewer points per 10 mph of wind; run-heavy teams score 0.9 fewer.
  • Rushing efficiency barely moves (-0.005 [-0.012, +0.002] EPA per rush per 10 mph). Turnovers show no clear change.
  • Field goals: holding kicker-season and distance fixed, 20+ mph winds lower the make rate by 3.8 percentage points relative to 0 to 9 mph games (95% CI 0.7 to 6.9). The extra effect at 40+ yards is not separately clear.
  • Market: combined scoring falls 2.5 points per 10 mph but the total line falls only 0.6, so games finish 1.9 points further under the line per 10 mph (p < 0.001). The gap appears in each of three eras.
  • This uses wind measured at the game, which a bettor only sees as a forecast. The market gap is an upper bound on what a forecast could capture.

What the total line does with wind

The line falls with wind, but not as far as scoring does. Under rates rise from the calm band to the 10–19 mph bands. The 20+ band is small (181 graded games) and noisy.

The spread shows a weaker version: when the home offense is the pass-heavier side, it does worse against the spread as wind rises (-1.06 [-1.84, -0.28] points per 10 mph per 10 pp identity gap, p = 0.011). With many tests in this study, treat that one as a lead, not a finding.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusCandidate for the combined model, not deployed. Wind and wind × pass identity go into the Phase 3 test against the closing total and spread, with a time-ordered holdout. Live use would rely on forecast wind, which needs its own historical forecast data before any adjustment can be validated.

Data and what was not available

Games
7,276 NFL games 1999–2025; 5,203 outdoor games with a wind reading; 1,815 dome or closed-roof games used as the no-wind reference in a robustness check.
Team-games
10,318 outdoor offense-games with a pre-game identity (8,595 from season-to-date, 1,723 from the prior season).
Weather
Stadium-reported temperature and wind (nflverse). Rain and snow parsed from the game weather text. Two readings above 45 mph were treated as errors.
Lines
nflverse total_line and spread_line for 5,152 games. Capture time is not documented; treated as a near-close consensus, not a licensed closing feed.
Field goals
19,666 outdoor attempts, play level, with kicker-season fixed effects.
Crosswind

Wind direction is a compass reading and stadium field orientation is not in the data, so crosswind versus headwind cannot be computed.

College football

No historical CFB game weather was reachable from this environment. The study is NFL only.

Wind coverage

Outdoor games with a wind reading dropped to 57% in 2022 and 84% in 2023, against 93–100% in other seasons.

Forecasts

The study uses measured game wind. Historical forecasts, which is what a bettor actually has, were not available.

Retractable roofs

128 games with an open retractable roof have no wind reading and were excluded.

Reproducible package: edgedesk-wind-offensive-identity.zip (scripts, game-level dataset, raw model output).
