Performance Intelligence · Combined Environment Model

One environmental adjustment, tested against the close

The seven studies are fit jointly so overlapping effects are not counted twice, then scored season by season on games the model never saw. The bar is simple: beat the betting line, not a weaker baseline.

Validation reportNFL 2011–2025 test seasons · CFB 2011–2019Updated 2026-10-17
Research questionWhen travel, rest, weather, wind, fatigue, officiating and altitude are fit together, does anything improve on the closing number out of sample?

Findings

  • NFL spreads, 2011–2025: all modules together change squared error vs the line by -0.37 (95% CI -0.79 to +0.04). No single module beats the line on its own.
  • CFB spreads, 2011–2019: all modules together -0.13 (95% CI -0.34 to +0.06). No single module beats the line on its own.
  • NFL totals: all modules together +0.45 (95% CI -0.23 to +1.13). Beat the total alone: Weather adaptation.
  • The one candidate: weather adaptation on NFL totals hit 57.8% of 697 out-of-sample bets (95% CI 54.3% to 61.4%). It uses weather recorded at kickoff, which the market only saw as a forecast, so this is an upper bound until it is retested on archived forecasts. It is also one of 18 single-module tests run here.
  • Adding modules one by one would double count: in the 1% of NFL games where stacked single-module adjustments are largest, they average 5.73 points against 4.78 from the joint fit.
  • Every adjustment is scored only on seasons the model never saw, with the penalty chosen inside the training seasons.

How the test works

The target is the market residual: final home margin minus the margin the closing spread implied (and, for NFL totals, total points minus the closing total). A feature only helps if it predicts that residual.

For each test season the model trains on every earlier season, picks a ridge penalty by time-ordered validation inside those training seasons, and predicts the test season. Features are pre-game knowable only; game-time weather is the one exception and is labeled as an upper bound because forecasts are noisier than the reading.

MSE gain is the market’s squared error minus the model’s, per game. Positive means the adjustment beat the line. Intervals bootstrap over season-weeks. The betting check takes the model’s side whenever its adjustment is at least one point; 52.4% is break-even at -110.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusNothing here changes EdgeDesk spreads. The weather-on-totals result is registered as a research candidate: it must be rebuilt on archived game-day forecasts and pass a preregistered forward window against closing totals before it can touch a fair total. Only a module whose out-of-sample interval sits above zero against the closing line is eligible to touch EdgeDesk fair numbers, and even then it enters as a registered research model with its own validation report and a preregistered evaluation window, not as a direct change to live spreads. Everything else stays as observed context on game research pages.

Data and what was not available

Spread target
Home margin minus market-implied margin.
NFL market
nflverse spread_line and total_line, 1999–2025 (capture time undocumented).
CFB market
SBR odds archive close, 2006–2019, verified venues.
Model
Ridge regression on standardized features with missing-value flags; rolling-origin season splits; penalty from time-ordered inner validation.
EdgeDesk model history

EdgeDesk has no multi-season archive of its own historical fair lines, so the baseline is the closing market line, which is the stronger benchmark.

Forecast weather

Historical game forecasts were not available; actual game weather overstates what was knowable pre-game.

CFB weather and officials

No historical CFB weather or crew data was reachable, so those modules are NFL only.
