Performance Intelligence · Defensive Fatigue

Do tired defenses break late?

Across 9,710 NFL and 16,299 college defensive performances, a big first-half workload does not make a defense worse after halftime once you account for who is playing and the score. The apparent "fatigue" in raw numbers is the reverse: busy defenses were having bad halves and simply regressed.

No effect after controlsNFL 1999–2025 regular season · CFB 2004–2025Updated 2026-10-17
Research questionDoes a defense that faces a heavy first-half workload become measurably more vulnerable in the second half and fourth quarter?

Findings

  • Raw numbers suggest defenses get better after a heavy first half: NFL defenses facing 40+ first-half snaps allowed +0.051 EPA/play before halftime and -0.014 after. That is regression to the mean. Defenses face many snaps because the opponent is moving the ball well, and that does not persist.
  • After controlling for first-half performance, halftime score, defense team-season and offense team-season, each extra 5 first-half snaps changes 2nd-half EPA allowed by +0.001 [-0.005, +0.007] in the NFL and +0.000 [-0.004, +0.005] in CFB. Both are near zero, and the intervals rule out anything larger than about 0.01 EPA per play.
  • Fourth-quarter EPA shows the same: -0.003 [-0.012, +0.006] (NFL) and -0.000 [-0.008, +0.007] (CFB) per 5 snaps.
  • Using workload created by the defense’s own offense going three-and-out (which adds 1.4 NFL and 1.2 CFB first-half snaps per three-and-out) gives 2nd-half effects of -0.003 [-0.026, +0.021] (NFL) and -0.016 [-0.040, +0.008] (CFB) per 5 snaps. Neither is distinguishable from zero.
  • Facing no-huddle tempo in the first half does not matter either: +0.005 [-0.007, +0.016] 2nd-half EPA per 5 no-huddle snaps faced (NFL).
  • Last week’s workload does not carry into this week: +0.002 [-0.003, +0.006] (NFL) and +0.002 [-0.001, +0.005] (CFB) EPA per play per 10 extra snaps faced in the previous game.
  • Against the market, last week’s workload gap does not predict covers (NFL -0.11 [-0.35, +0.13] pts per 10 snaps, p = 0.37; CFB -0.21 [-0.45, +0.03], p = 0.09). Combined previous-game snaps lean toward the over by +0.32 points per 10 snaps (p = 0.03), a single result among about 30 tests that needs out-of-sample confirmation.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusNot added to the model. First-half workload, tempo faced and last week’s workload show no measurable effect on later defensive performance after controls, in either the NFL or college football. The previous-game workload fields are kept as observed context and passed to the combined model, where the small totals lean will be tested out of sample before anything changes.

Data and what was not available

NFL
nflverse play-by-play, regular season 1999–2025; 13,928 defense-games built, 9,710 with at least 10 competitive plays in each half.
CFB
ESPN play-by-play via sportsdataverse, 2004–2025; 30,526 defense-games built, 16,299 used.
Workload
Opponent scrimmage plays faced (team-level snaps), opponent drives of 10+ plays, own-offense three-and-outs, no-huddle snaps faced (NFL).
Market
NFL: nflverse spread and total lines. CFB: SBR archive spreads 2006–2019 (Pinnacle where present). Neither is a licensed closing feed.
Missed tackles

Not recorded in any public play-by-play, so not measured.

Player snap counts

Available for the NFL only from 2012; this study uses team-level snaps faced. Rotation depth was not tested.

Second-half lines

No historical 2H or live lines, so the market test uses full-game spreads and totals with pre-game workload.

Time between drives

Real-time rest between a defense’s series is not reliably recorded; own-offense three-and-outs stand in for short rest.

Reproducible package: edgedesk-defensive-fatigue.zip (scripts, game-level dataset, raw model output).
