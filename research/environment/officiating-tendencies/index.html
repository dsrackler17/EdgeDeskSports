Referee crews change the flag count, not the score

Across 7,272 NFL games from 1999 to 2025, the referee in charge moves the penalty count by a small, repeatable amount. That tendency does not show up in points scored, totals or covers.

Real for flags, not for scoresNFL · 1999–2025 · 42 referees with 40+ gamesUpdated 2026-10-17
Research questionDo NFL officiating crews introduce measurable variation beyond the teams themselves, and does it reach totals or spreads?

Findings

  • Crew differences in total flags are real. After removing both teams’ season-level tendencies, the spread between referees is about 0.52 flags per game (one standard deviation) against a league average of 12.5. Shuffling referee labels within seasons never produces a spread that large (permutation p = 0.001).
  • The tendency repeats: a referee’s rate in odd seasons correlates 0.51 with his rate in even seasons (95% CI 0.30 to 0.71); season to season it is 0.26.
  • The repeatable part is mostly offensive holding (split-half r 0.57), personal fouls (0.56) and roughing the passer (0.49). Defensive pass interference shows no reliable crew pattern (r 0.17, p = 0.17).
  • No crew treats road teams measurably differently. Home teams draw 0.40 fewer flags per game league-wide, but the referee-level spread in that gap is indistinguishable from chance (p = 0.23, split-half r 0.03).
  • A crew’s prior-season tendency does predict this season’s flags (+0.84 [+0.57, +1.11] flags per flag of prior tendency), but not total points (-0.13 [-1.07, +0.80]) or the result against the total (+0.08 [-0.56, +0.72]).
  • Games with high-flag crews went over 49.6% of the time versus 49.3% for low-flag crews. Home teams covered 50.6% with crews that historically flag home teams more versus 47.9% with the opposite; neither gap is distinguishable from zero.

How much of the flag count belongs to the crew

Each game’s penalty count is first adjusted for both teams’ season-long tendencies and for the season itself, so a crew is not credited for working a lot of undisciplined teams. The remaining variation is split into a referee part and game-to-game noise. The permutation test reshuffles referees within each season 1,000 times to see how much referee spread chance alone produces.

Does the market miss it?

For every game from 2001 on, each referee’s tendency is computed from earlier seasons only, as it would have been known when the assignment was announced. Games are then split into thirds by that prior tendency.

High-flag crews call about 1.0 more flags per game than low-flag crews, yet total points move by -0.4 and the closing total by +0.0. Extra flags do not translate into extra points on average, which is why the market has nothing to price.

Pass-heavy games

Pass-defense flags (pass interference, defensive holding, illegal contact, roughing the passer) were compared in games above and below the season’s median dropback rate. Referee effects in the two sets correlate 0.60 across 41 referees, and the spread between referees is somewhat wider in pass-heavy games (0.24 vs 0.17 flags, raw). Crews that call these fouls tightly do so regardless of game style, with a little more room to show it when there are more dropbacks.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusNot added to the model. Crew flag tendency is real and repeatable, so the assigned referee and his prior-season flag rate can appear as observed context on NFL game research. It does not predict points, totals or covers out of sample, so it does not move any fair line, probability or grade.

Data and what was not available

Games
7,272 NFL regular-season and playoff games 1999–2025 with a named referee (89 referees; 42 with 40+ games).
Penalties
Penalty plays recorded in nflverse play-by-play, attributed to the penalized team; type from the play-by-play penalty_type field.
Market lines
nflverse spread_line and total_line, tested on 5,892 games where the referee had 16+ earlier games. nflverse does not document capture time; treated as near-close consensus, not a licensed closing feed.
Crew
The referee identifies the crew. Full seven-person crews exist only from 2015 and rotate partially, so they were not modeled separately.
College football

No public game-level officiating crew data for FBS was available, so this study is NFL only.

Declined and offsetting flags

Play-by-play does not record every declined or offsetting flag consistently, so counts reflect recorded penalties.

Crew members

Umpire, line judges and other crew members can matter for specific fouls; only the referee is modeled.
