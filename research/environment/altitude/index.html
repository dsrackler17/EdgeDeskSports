Thin air, thin edge: altitude and FBS road teams

Across 13,013 venue-verified FBS road games, visitors facing a big climb lose by about a point more than their ratings suggest, with no late-game fade, and the closing spread already accounts for it.

Priced by the marketCFB · FBS vs FBS · 2001–2025Updated 2026-10-17
Research questionDo visitors who climb 3,000+ feet to play fade late, cover less, or defend worse than other road teams?

Findings

  • Raw numbers point the wrong way: 3,000 ft+ visitors lost by 2.1 points less than other visitors, because high-altitude hosts are weaker on average.
  • After team-season strength and travel, rest, dome, capacity, pace and season controls, 3,000 ft+ visitors do -1.2 points worse (95% CI -1.19 [-2.71, +0.33]), wild-bootstrap p = 0.07.
  • No fade: the effect is -0.55 [-2.01, +0.91] in the second half and -0.85 [-1.95, +0.25] in the fourth quarter, about the same as the first half.
  • Against the closing spread, 3,000 ft+ visitors covered 49.5% of 410 games versus 51.5% for low-gain visitors; adjusted ATS margin -0.99 [-3.09, +1.10].
  • Third downs, second-half explosives, penalties, plays faced and turnovers show no 3,000 ft+ effect after controls.
  • The 6× elevation-ratio flag adds nothing once absolute climb is modeled (+0.64 [-0.62, +1.91] points); it mostly marks visitors from sea-level stadiums.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusNot added to the model. The adjusted effect is about one point, its interval includes zero, and closing spreads already price it. Host elevation and visitor climb will show as observed context on CFB game research, with no effect on the fair spread unless the combined model finds incremental value against the close.
