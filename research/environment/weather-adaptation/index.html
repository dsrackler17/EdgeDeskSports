Weather shock: do road teams struggle in conditions they never play in?

Across 5,146 NFL games at outdoor stadiums, a visitor playing colder than its own normal does slightly worse, and the spread does not fully adjust. The effect is small, it mostly comes from the last 13 seasons, and it needs an out-of-sample test before it can mean anything.

Two candidates, unvalidatedNFL · outdoor games 2000–2025Updated 2026-10-17
Research questionDoes an unusually cold, hot, humid or wet road game hurt a team more when it is far from the conditions it normally plays in?

Findings

  • Each 10°F a game is colder than the visitor’s own normal for that month costs the visitor -0.71 points (95% CI -1.35 to -0.07, wild-bootstrap p = 0.04) after team-season strength, generic temperature, wind, rain, division and season controls.
  • The spread misses most of it: visitor margin against the number moves -0.69 points per 10°F of cold shock (95% CI -1.35 to -0.02, p = 0.04). The estimate is -0.25 in 2000–2012 and -1.00 in 2013–2025, so it is not stable across eras.
  • The famous version, a warm-climate team in a freezing road game, looks bad in raw numbers: warm-climate visitors covered 42.2% of 187 games at 40°F or colder (95% CI 36.2% to 47.7%) versus 49.3% for cold-climate visitors. After controls the class effect is -0.96 [-2.63, +0.72] points, p = 0.22, so the raw gap is mostly team quality and luck.
  • Dome teams are not hurt in cold games overall (-0.16 [-2.39, +2.07] points), but their field-goal percentage changes by -6.7 [-13.0, -0.4] percentage points at 40°F or colder.
  • Early-season heat shows nothing: +0.60 [-1.37, +2.57] points per 10°F warmer than the visitor’s normal in August and September (169 games at 85°F or hotter).
  • Rain matters for totals, not sides. Games with rain or snow noted at kickoff went over 42.0% of the time (95% CI 37.9% to 46.2%, n = 371) versus 49.3% in dry games, finishing -2.48 [-4.53, -0.42] points against the closing total. The precipitation flag comes from kickoff conditions, which the market only knew as a forecast.

How a team’s normal is defined

For every team and season, EdgeDesk takes the kickoff temperatures of that team’s outdoor home games in the same calendar month over the previous five seasons (at least three games, otherwise the same part of the season). Nothing from the current season or the game itself is used, so the figure was knowable before kickoff.

Teams that played at least half their home games indoors over the previous three seasons are classed as dome teams. Outdoor teams whose late-season home normal is 55°F or warmer are warm-climate teams. Everyone else is a cold-climate team. Cold shock is how many degrees colder the game is than the visitor’s normal for that month.

Stability check

The two effects that survived controls were re-estimated separately on each half of the sample. An effect that only exists in one era is more likely to be noise or a market that has since changed.

Roughly 50 coefficients are reported across this study, so one or two with p near 0.04 are expected by chance. Neither effect is validated until the combined model tests it out of sample.

What this means for the EdgeDesk model

Model statusNot added to the model yet. Cold shock against the visitor’s normal and kickoff rain on totals are the two candidates. Both go into the combined environment model, which tests them on later seasons it never trained on and against the closing number. Until then they appear in game research as observed context only: game temperature, the visitor’s normal for that month, and the forecast.

Data and what was not available

Games
5,146 NFL games at outdoor or open-roof stadiums, 2000–2025, where both teams had at least three prior seasons of home history. Neutral-site games excluded.
Weather
Kickoff temperature, wind and conditions from the NFL gamebook via nflverse. 236 games with a blank temperature field (mostly 2022–2023) were filled from the same gamebook text, which matches the field within 1°F on 96.7% of games where both exist.
Lines
nflverse spread and total for every game. nflverse does not document when the line was captured, so it is treated as a near-close consensus, not a licensed closing feed.
Play-by-play
nflverse play-by-play, 10,290 team-games, with franchise codes matched to the schedule (checked after a shared-layer fix for OAK/SD/STL).
College football

No historical CFB game weather was reachable, so this study is NFL only.

Forecast vs actual

Weather is what was recorded at kickoff. The market priced the forecast, which is very close for temperature and much less reliable for rain.

Practice climate

A team’s normal is built from its home games. Where it practices, including indoor facilities, is not recorded.

Heat index and dew point

Humidity exists from 2001 on, but only 73 games were 80°F+ with 70%+ humidity, too few to model separately.

Wind direction

Recorded as a compass point without the field’s orientation, so crosswind cannot be computed.
