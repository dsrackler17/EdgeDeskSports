## 1. The question, and the answer

The question was never "does altitude matter". It was: **does accounting for
altitude make EdgeDesk's CFB predictions measurably better than they already
are?**

**No, not on the evidence available.**
- Conditional on V1's own pregame number, home teams hosting a visitor that
  climbed 3,000+ ft beat V1 by **+1.03 pts** more than other home teams.
  - SE 0.62, 95% CI −0.18 to +2.23, wild-cluster bootstrap p = 0.14.
  - 13,511 non-neutral FBS home games, 2006–2025; 651 of them treated, at 14
    host venues.
- Chosen without looking at 2022–2025 and then scored on it, the adjustment
  made the 139 games it touched slightly worse:
  - MAE 12.905 → 12.924; improvement −0.019, 95% CI −0.079 to +0.046, paired
    p = 0.55.
- Shuffling the altitude features produced a result at least this good 66% of
  the time (permutation p = 0.66).

The baseline-preserving fallback therefore stands.

**Why an additive term would double count.** On average, V1 over-credits the
home side by 1.26 pts: its 4.082-pt league home-field constant is too large.
At 3,000+ ft venues its error is about zero (−0.30). So the "altitude effect" a
regression finds is mostly the gap between those two. Adding it to V1, without
also fixing V1's home-field constant, pushes mountain home teams *past* where V1
already has them. That is what the confirmation window shows: the bias on
affected games went from +0.12 to −0.23.

The general home lean is the job of the football-only margin calibrator
(`football/cfb_p4/margin_calibration.js`, already in SHADOW), not of an altitude
term.

## 2. The research package, re-verified

`altitude_analysis.zip` was treated as research input, not production code.
Every headline number was re-read from its own `results.json`:

| claim | verified value |
|---|---|
| 3,000+ ft vs <1,000 ft, visitor margin, team-season FE + controls | **−1.191** (SE 0.774), n = 10,745, 149 venue clusters, 532 treated; wild-cluster p = 0.072 |
| 6× ratio, added to the same model | **+0.644** (SE 0.647), i.e. no independent penalty; wild p = 0.27 |
| fourth quarter, 3,000+ ft | −0.850 (SE 0.563), wild p = 0.092: suggestive, unproven |
| second half, 3,000+ ft | −0.549 (SE 0.746), wild p = 0.43 |
| against the spread (Pinnacle close, 2006–2019) | −0.995 (SE 1.067), wild p = 0.29 |
| all seasons 2001–2025 (venues inferred 2022+) | −1.721 (SE 0.678) |

What the package's own definitions do, and what this module does instead:

- **The `<1,000 ft` reference bucket mixes descents with level trips.** A
  Colorado team flying to Miami and Purdue driving to Indiana were the same
  group. Here a descent is its own bucket, and it is used as a placebo (§5).
- **The 6× ratio mostly flags trips that are not altitude.** 59% of the
  package's 6× games gained less than 1,000 ft: sea-level visitors reach 6× at
  a 60 ft venue. The ratio is computed for description only and never prices.
- **Time-zone offsets ignored daylight saving.** Arizona ↔ California is 0
  hours in September, not 1. Indiana had no DST before 2006. Both are handled
  here.
- **Season openers were silently dropped.** A missing previous game made rest
  NaN, and the regression dropped the row. Here rest is "unavailable" for an
  opener and nothing else is lost.
- **Team-season fixed effects use results from after each game.** That is
  ex-post research, not a pregame model. This module fits only on V1's pregame
  residuals, and only on seasons before the one it predicts.

### Data and features (`engine.js`, the same function in research and live)

- **A game is located by the schedule's own `venue_id`**, never by the home
  team, so neutral sites, stadium moves and relocated home games are placed
  correctly. With no venue id on a home game, it is placed at the home team's
  normal venue, and the section says so.
- **A team's usual elevation** is its normal home venue that season: the modal
  non-neutral home `venue_id` among its scheduled home games. It falls back to
  the previous season, then to the listed venue, and says which it used.
- **Measured per side:**
  - game elevation;
  - usual elevation;
  - signed gain, and positive gain;
  - direction (gain / none / loss, ±100 ft);
  - buckets: large descent, descent, level, moderate gain 1,000–2,999, high
    gain 3,000+;
  - venue class: low, moderate, high, very high;
  - the ratio (descriptive only);
  - great-circle miles from the usual venue;
  - signed time-zone change at kickoff, DST-correct;
  - rest days within the season;
  - two acclimatization proxies the schedule supports: the team lives at
    3,000+ ft, and its previous game (within 14 days) was at 3,000+ ft.
- **Neutral sites** are described for both teams. The focus is the team that
  climbed more. No adjustment is ever made: none was estimated.
- **Missing is missing.** A venue without a sourced elevation makes the context
  unusable. The page says "unavailable", the desk says "elevation unavailable",
  and nothing is imputed.

### The venue registry (`venues.json`, 326 venues: 298 HIGH, 20 MEDIUM, 8 LOW)

- **Primary source:** the cfbfastR team tables, which republish the
  CollegeFootballData venue table, elevation in metres. The trained V1 table is
  a cross-check. The research package fills only historic stadiums today's
  snapshot no longer lists (Hughes, Sam Boyd, Aloha, Qualcomm, …), at MEDIUM.
- **Found and fixed: inherited geography.** The provider attaches each team's
  *current* stadium geography to whatever venue id its row carries. The 2026
  table lists Colorado State at Hughes Stadium (closed 2016) with Canvas
  Stadium's coordinates and elevation, and UNLV at Sam Boyd with Allegiant's
  2,185 ft.
  - When venue ids share one provider coordinate pair, the schedule decides
    which one is current.
  - Legacy ids keep an elevation only from an independent capture (MEDIUM), or
    are LOW and never adjust anything.
  - Hughes is placed at 5,167 ft, not Canvas's 5,014 ft. Sam Boyd is placed at
    1,608 ft, not 2,185 ft.
- Confidence: HIGH means sources agree within 100 ft; MEDIUM means a single
  secondary capture, or sources 100–300 ft apart; LOW means sources more than
  300 ft apart, or a legacy id with no independent capture.

### Estimation (`research/run.js`)

- **Baseline:** the shipped V1 engine, replayed cold in kickoff order from 2001
  by the existing `football/cfb_p4/research/replay_rows.js`. It was reused,
  with only additive fields (venue, neutral, total, win probability).
  - 14,213 FBS-vs-FBS games projected.
- **Residual:** final home margin minus V1's pregame fair margin.
  - Every candidate includes an intercept that absorbs V1's general home lean.
  - The intercept is never part of the adjustment.
- **Candidates, pre-registered in `engine.js` CANDIDATES before any fit:**
  - continuous per 1,000 ft;
  - piecewise with knots at 1,000 and 3,000 ft;
  - categorical 3,000+;
  - the package's two-bucket specification;
  - 3,000+ interacted with resident altitude, recent altitude exposure, and
    V1's pregame total (a pace proxy that exists live).
  - Two diagnostics that can never price: 3,000+ interacted with visitor
    defensive play load and with opponent pace, each with its main effect.
    Play counts exist only through 2021 and have no live input.
  - The ratio and a descent placebo.
- **Inference:** OLS with standard errors clustered by host venue. The main
  term gets a wild-cluster bootstrap-t (999 draws).
- **Estimate:** ridge, with the penalty chosen by forward-chaining inside the
  training seasons, times an uncertainty shrink of t²/(1+t²).
- **Minimum support:** a term on fewer than 30 games or 5 venues is held at 0.
  This rule was added after a dry run exposed 2-game interaction cells; it can
  only remove a term.
- **Walk-forward:** each season 2014–2025 is predicted from a fit on 2006 up to
  the season before, and the harness asserts it.
  - The candidate is selected on 2014–2021 only.
  - 2022–2025 is the confirmation window.
- **Double counting:** the host-only, host + gain and host + gain + travel fits
  are reported (BACKTEST.md §Double counting).

### Backtests and gates (`research/gates.js`, declared 2026-10-17 before the first fit)

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| G1 the repository's own promotion rules (`football/validation/promote.js`) on the affected confirmation games | **FAIL**: MAE worse by 0.019, p = 0.55 |
| G2 ≥ 100 affected confirmation games | pass (139) |
| G3 no harm to all games (MAE ≤ +0.002, Brier ≤ +0.0005) | pass (+0.001, 0.0000) |
| G4 permutation placebo p < 0.05 | **FAIL** (p = 0.66, 200 shuffles) |
| G5 descent placebo CI includes 0 | pass (+0.82, CI −0.13 to +1.77) |
| G6 venue robustness (no sign flip; held-out venues improve) | **FAIL**: no flips, but held-out mountain venues got worse (−0.049) |
| G7 coefficient sign stable in ≥ 80% of refits | pass (12/12) |
| G8 ≥ 95% of confirmation games usable | pass (100%) |

Reported in BACKTEST.md:
- MAE, RMSE and bias for every window and subgroup;
- Brier, log loss and ECE;
- ATS against the archive close: 53.7% on 136 affected confirmation games, for
  both arms, because the adjustment never flipped a side;
- opener-to-close movement: 53.9% of 317 moved lines went toward the altitude
  side, +0.19 pts on average;
- 95% intervals by host-venue cluster bootstrap;
- the 3,000+ ft and 6× subsets separately;
- leave-one-mountain-venue-out.

**The market at altitude.** On 586 3,000+ ft visits with a close, home teams
beat the closing number by +1.40 pts (95% CI +0.31 to +2.59, but over only 14
venues). Visitors covered 47.3% of 577 decided games, which is not
distinguishable from 50% (p = 0.20). The package's fixed-effects ATS estimate
was not significant either. This is a monitoring item, not an edge, and nothing
in the product says otherwise.

**A lead that is not altitude.** The visitor defence's season-to-date play
load has a main effect of +0.98 pts per 10 plays (SE 0.28). V1 does not price
it, and it improved the 2014–2021 walk-forward by 0.008 MAE. Play counts here
end in 2021, so it cannot be confirmed or priced from this package. It belongs
to V1's own research queue, not to this module.

## 4. Product integration

- **Research page** (`#game/cfb/<id>`, `lib/edgedesk_game_view.js`
  altitudeSection), compact. It shows:
  - venue, normal and elevation-change tiles;
  - the trip (miles, zones, rest);
  - a plain-language interpretation;
  - **Statistical support / Validated model adjustment / Impact on EdgeDesk's
    number** ("0.0 pts pending validation");
  - "Data and method", with the shadow estimate labelled NOT in the number,
    the ratio labelled descriptive, the acclimatization proxies, sources,
    anything unavailable, and what is not claimed.
  - Tested in Chromium at 390 px and 1280 px (`ui.e2e.js`).
- **Snapshot** (`lib/edgedesk_game_snapshot.js` altitudeBlock): the block is
  added only when the slate carries it, so snapshots built before it keep their
  ids. An applied number is believed only if the engine's own contribution
  agrees with it.
- **V1 engine:** a tenth term, `altitude exposure`, is computed on every game.
  - It is `M.missing` (0 points, with the shadow number in its reason) unless
    `params.js` is VALIDATED with points_applied AND the request's adjustment
    is APPLIED, and never at a neutral site.
  - Explanation text exists for the day it is applied.
- **Slate build** (`football/fbs/build_coverage.js`): computes the context from
  the season's own schedule rows, reads the flag fail-closed, hands the
  adjustment to the engine, and publishes `altitude_travel` with the engine's
  contribution beside it.
  - **Proof of no change:** the same offline build with and without this code
    gave identical margin, line, total, win probability and status on all 108
    games of the 2026-10-17 slate.
- **Ask EdgeDesk:**
  - The FBS slate normaliser (`_lib.ts`) carries the section.
  - `altitudeSituation()` fills the packet's travel and altitude slots with
    sourced facts and the verdict, never the shadow number.
  - The desk's Situation panel shows an Altitude cell.
  - The explanation boundary (`_cfb_explain.js`) refuses "altitude", "elevation"
    or "thin air" unless the facts carry the section. It refuses any number the
    section does not carry and any promise.
  - The research terminal passes the section through to that boundary.
- **Registry of modules** (`lib/edgedesk_canon.js`): `altitude_travel` is
  SHADOW with pricing impact NO. "Altitude exposure" is listed among the
  unpriced terms.

## 5. Safe release

- **Three locks:**
  1. `params.js` status VALIDATED, which only a research run that passes every
     gate can write;
  2. `points_applied: true`, written by that same run;
  3. the guarded server flag `cfb_altitude_adjustment_enabled`.
- **The flag:**
  - It is declared OFF by `supabase/cfb_altitude.sql`.
  - It changes only through `cfb_set_feature_flag`, which refuses automation
    and refuses no evidence. Every change is audited.
  - `football/altitude/flag.js` reads it **fail-closed**: no credentials, an
    unseeded flag, an error or a timeout all mean off.
- **The engine and the SQL table re-check the locks:**
  - A forged "APPLIED" request moves nothing.
  - A row with points but without the three locks is refused by a check
    constraint.
- **Shadow:** every game's adjustment is computed, published and mirrored
  (`cfb_altitude_shadow`, append-only, written before kickoff).
  - `monitor.js` grades the frozen candidate on games the fit never saw.
  - On the 2026 season so far: 12 affected games, −0.10 MAE. That is too few to
    read, and the monitor says so.
- **Activation checklist** (in order; skipping a step leaves the module in
  shadow, which is the safe failure):
  1. Rerun `npm run cfb:altitude:research` on a new season of data.
     `params.js` must come out VALIDATED with every gate passing.
  2. Review BACKTEST.md and `monitoring.json`'s prospective record. A person
     decides.
  3. Give **every** slate build step `SB_URL`/`SB_SERVICE_ROLE`, so all of them
     read the same flag:
     - `football-weekly-build.yml`,
     - `football-enrichment.yml`,
     - `starter-context.yml`.
     With only some, the builds would disagree.
  4. `select public.cfb_set_feature_flag('cfb_altitude_adjustment_enabled', true, '<person>', '<reason>', '<evidence ref>');`
  5. Watch `monitoring.json` (CRITICAL on any disagreement) and
     `cfb_altitude_monitor`.
- **Rollback:** [`ROLLBACK.md`](ROLLBACK.md).

## 7. Outstanding limitations

- **Venue elevations.**
  - The USGS point service could not be reached, so research-package values
    were not independently re-verified. They are MEDIUM.
  - Northwestern's 2026 temporary venue (id 11823) has no sourced elevation, so
    its games show "unavailable".
  - Many neutral NFL stadiums are absent from the provider table.
- **Historic schedule rows.** 217 non-neutral games (1.6%), mostly 2006–2010,
  have no venue in the feed. They are excluded rather than placed at a stadium
  the team moved to later.
- **The line archive** carries no per-quote timestamps. "Close" is the
  archive's final consensus number; openers are thin before 2013. The licence
  terms of the underlying sportsbook data are not stated by the archive.
- **The baseline replay** carries no quarterback, injury or weather inputs (none
  exist historically). That is how V1 is replayed everywhere in this
  repository.
- **Neutral sites** are described, not modelled.
- **Acclimatization** is two schedule proxies. No travel-timing, training or
  physiological data exists.
- **The research-terminal card** (`app.html` fbP4Card) does not yet render the
  section; the research page and the desk do. The terminal object now carries
  the section for the explanation boundary.
- **`football/cfb_v2`** (the shadow challenger) still uses its own team-keyed
  `altitude_kft`. Moving it onto this registry belongs to its next retrain.
- **Fourth-quarter fades, missed tackles, penalties and turnovers** were not
  modelled, and nothing claims them.
